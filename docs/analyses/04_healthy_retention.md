\# Analysis 4 – Healthy retention (casino)



\## Question

How much of the retention we see in month 3 comes from players who showed

high-intensity spending in their first 30 days?



\## Hypothesis (written before running any query, 7 Nov 2026)

Early high-intensity players make up more than 30% of the new casino players

who are still active in month 3.



\## Definitions

\- Population: new casino players with at least one approved deposit into gambling,

&#x20; cohorts May–Nov 2019 (so month 3 is inside the data).

\- Early high-intensity: more than $1,038 of approved deposits into gambling in the

&#x20; first 30 days after the first deposit ($1,038 = 90th percentile of monthly deposit

&#x20; amount among repeat depositors, Analysis 3).

\- Other depositors: everyone else in the population.

\- Active in month k = at least one approved deposit into gambling k months after

&#x20; the cohort month (decision 13).

\- Measure for the hypothesis: share of month-3 active players who are early high-intensity.

\- Also reported (not part of the hypothesis): month 1–3 retention per group, and the

&#x20; share of month-3 deposit money from each group.

\- "High-intensity" is a spending proxy, not confirmed harm.



\## Result

| Group | Players | % of players | Retention M1 | M2 | M3 | Active in M3 | % of M3 active | % of M3 money |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Early high-intensity | 109 | 12.2 | 76.1% | 52.3% | 45.9% | 50 | 30.9 | 63.3 |

| Other depositors | 788 | 87.8 | 21.1% | 15.1% | 14.2% | 112 | 69.1 | 36.7 |



\- Early high-intensity players are about 3x more likely to be active in month 3 (45.9% vs 14.2%).

\- 12% of new depositors make up 31% of month-3 active players and 63% of month-3 deposit money.



\## Confirmed or rejected?

Confirmed, but narrowly: 30.9% vs a 30% threshold. With only 50 early high-intensity

players active in month 3, the margin is smaller than normal chance variation.

The robust finding is the size of the gap (3x retention, 63% of month-3 money),

not the exact threshold.



\## Recommendation

Report retention separately for early high-intensity players, so that retention targets

do not reward high-intensity play. Use first-30-day spending as one early signal for

a Duty of Care review, alongside other markers.



\## Open questions

\- Which first-30-day signals beyond spending (frequency, declines, time of day) separate the groups?
